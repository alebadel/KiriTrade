What this page shows, and why it matters

The Western and Central Pacific Fisheries Commission (WCPFC) keeps a public Record of Fishing Vessels. Each member state submits the vessels it authorises to fish in the Commission's area, with the vessel's type, size, build year, previous flag, and the name and address of its owner. The tracker reads the entries for the Kiribati, Nauru and Tuvalu flags. Nauru and Tuvalu are included because the ship-tracking source sometimes reports a vessel under a neighbouring flag.

The register matters for two reasons. First, it says which hulls the flag can be credited with, so the ship-tracking page counts only vessels on it and only during their time on it. Second, the owner's address is the only public hint about where a vessel's operator is based. That is the hinge of the whole question: importers credit a fish to the vessel's flag, while the balance of payments credits a sale to the operator's country of residence. An address is not residence. A company with a Kiribati address can be run from abroad, and a foreign address can belong to a resident's agent. The tracker treats the address as a hint and the agents treat residence as a condition, never as a fact.

Where the owner's address points

The register's owner, operator and charterer fields were read for an address pattern. A match to a Kiribati, Nauru or Tuvalu address is a hint that the operator is based at home; an address abroad is a hint that it is based elsewhere. Many entries give no usable address.

What the register does not tell

It does not record who operates the vessel day to day, who owns the catch when it is sold, or where the operator is resident. It does not record catch, landings or value. It is maintained by the flag states, so an entry reflects what the state submitted and when; vessels can be on the register without fishing and can fish without a current entry. The tracker makes no statement about any vessel's ownership or residence; it counts what the register says.
