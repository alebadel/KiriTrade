The collection

Why the agents need these texts

The difference between the partner figure and the official figure is a difference of rules. The agents must therefore know the rules before they can reason about the numbers. Four kinds of text carry them. The statistical manuals (the IMF's Balance of Payments Manual and its compilation guide, the United Nations' International Merchandise Trade Statistics manuals) define what an export is and whose it is. The customs rules (the Kyoto Convention's annex on origin, the World Customs Organization's guidance on fish, Thailand's notifications and certificates of origin) say which country an importer credits a fish to. The data documentation from UN Comtrade says how each reporting country records its partners. The research, academic papers and IMF reports on partner ("mirror") data, says what has been learnt from comparing the two records elsewhere.

Two kinds of text are missing and the chart shows the gap. No news has been collected, so changes in fleets, licences or buyers reach the agents only through the data. And apart from the author's own briefs, there is no country document for Kiribati itself: no fisheries law, licensing rule or compiler's note. Adding either does not change the method; it changes what the agents know.
