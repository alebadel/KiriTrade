The estimate of true exports

The Inference page estimates the FOB value of tuna sold to non-residents in a calendar year by vessel operators resident in Kiribati. Five steps.

  1. Evidence pack. Every agent receives the same files: the monthly partner-reported tuna series (UN Comtrade, importers' records coded to Kiribati by vessel flag); fleet aggregates (fishing hours by register address pattern, the share of encounters followed by a carrier call at a Thai port, the median lag); annual partner-reported tuna for seven Pacific flag states; extracts from the statistical standards (BPM6 on residence and change of ownership, IMTS 2010 and the Kyoto Convention on origin, Thai customs origin rules); and Kiribati's official statistics. No vessel, company or personal names; no news.
  2. Instructions. The agent is told what true exports means and that the official figures are evidence of what the compiler recorded, not the target. It writes four to eight numbered principles, each with a source and an uncertainty, freezes them, then builds one bridge per year from the raw partner figure in six signed layers in a fixed order (the layers are listed on the Inference page). Each layer carries an 80% range and a rationale that cites principles and evidence files. The agent then audits its own answer. Words that impute dishonest intent to a reporter or compiler are banned, residence must be stated as a condition, and fishing licence fees may not be compared with the tuna series, because fees are income for access, not payment for fish (tracker/agents/bridge_prompt.md).
  3. Run. Thirty agents run in separate sessions with the same model and prompt (run of 20 October 2026: Claude Sonnet 5.5 at its default reasoning effort; recorded in data/inference.json for every run). A program checks every answer: six layers in order, start plus layers equal to the estimate within one cent, ranges ordered, no banned words, at least three principles. Rejected answers are listed with the reason and not replaced.
  4. Aggregation. For each year and layer the program computes the mean, standard deviation, median, 10th and 90th percentiles, minimum and maximum across accepted agents, and the same for the end value. The waterfall shows means and the 10th to 90th percentiles. The agents do no arithmetic that is published (tracker/build_inference_data.py).
  5. What the spread means. It is disagreement given identical evidence and one underlying model: not a standard error, not a confidence interval. Blind spots all agents share do not appear in it. The estimate is not scored against the official figure, which the Kiribati National Statistics Office compiles from its own returns on different evidence; its credibility rests on the written principles and on the validation test.

Terms used on the site

True exports
The FOB value of tuna sold to non-residents by vessel operators resident in Kiribati, whatever the vessel's flag or the route.
Balance of payments (BOP), BPM6
The official account of a country's transactions with the rest of the world, compiled under the IMF's Balance of Payments Manual, sixth edition. A good is an export when its ownership passes from a resident to a non-resident.
Residence versus flag
The flag is the country whose register a vessel is on. Residence is where the vessel's operator has its centre of economic interest. Importers credit fish to the flag; the balance of payments credits the sale to the operator's residence.
FOB, CIF
FOB values a good at the port of loading; CIF adds freight and insurance to the importer's port. Importers' records are CIF; the estimate is FOB.
Partner-reported, mirror data
The other side's record of the same shipments: what Kiribati's trading partners declared to UN Comtrade.
Flag basis
Tuna credited to Kiribati because the catching vessel flew the Kiribati flag, as importers record it.
AIS
The position beacon ships broadcast, collected by Global Fishing Watch.
WCPFC
The Western and Central Pacific Fisheries Commission, which keeps the public Record of Fishing Vessels.
HS codes
The Harmonized System, the product codes every customs service uses; six digits are the finest level shared by all countries.
Agents
The thirty separate sessions of a large language model that produce the estimate; the home page calls them the experts.
10th to 90th percentile
The range that holds the middle 80% of the agents' answers.
Baseline, pseudo-real-time test
A simple statistical forecast, tested by forecasting each past month with only the data published at the time.

Partner data: what they are

Every shipment is recorded twice: by the country it leaves and by the country it enters. Both records go to UN Comtrade. Partner-reported ("mirror") data are the other side of Kiribati's shipments: what Thailand recorded as imports from Kiribati, what Australia recorded as exports to Kiribati. The two records differ even when both are right: importers add freight and insurance, the sides can book a shipment in different months or product codes, and fish is coded to the flag of the catching vessel.

What is and is not comparable

Comparable: annual totals by broad product group once percent complete is high; fish exports over twelve-month windows. With care: quarters, and any period below about 70 percent complete. Not comparable: single products in single months, and monthly ratios, which balloon when the Kiribati figure is near zero. Partner-reported imports cover only part of what Kiribati buys, so no trade balance is computed from partner data. Fishing licence fees are income, a separate flow, and are never compared with the tuna series.

The partner panel, percent complete and lags

The tracker watches a fixed panel of partners: any partner with at least half a percent of Kiribati's partner-reported exports or imports over 2023 to 2025, plus sixteen regional and global partners always included (tracker/panel.json). Percent complete is the share of panel partners that have published a period, by count and weighted by each partner's trade share. Most partners publish within about four months; some publish only annually. Kiribati's own returns are missing for some months; a missing month is never drawn as zero.

How products are grouped

Tuna: HS6 030341 to 030349, 030487 and 160414. Fish: HS chapter 03 plus heading 1604. Copra oil: 151311 and 151319. Ships and aircraft: chapters 88 and 89. Fuel: chapter 27. Food: chapters 01 to 24 excluding 03.

The daily update

Once a day an automated job asks UN Comtrade for newly published months, rebuilds the partner-data files and republishes the site. If Comtrade is unavailable the previous files stay. The register, the knowledge base and the agent runs are refreshed by hand (tracker/README.md).

The early estimate of the partner series

The Partner data page carries a smaller product: an early estimate of the flag-basis partner figure for months not yet published. For a month with some returns, the baseline adds the non-Thai returns already published to the average of Thailand's last twelve published months; for months with none it uses the trailing twelve-month total. Its range is the 10th to 90th percentile of its own errors in a pseudo-real-time test over 86 months (March 2019 to May 2026). In that test the baseline cut the three-month error of repeating the same month of the previous year by about 40% (6.5 against 10.9, USD millions). A version that added carrier arrivals at Thai ports gave 6.4, not a significant gain, and the pre-set rule was that ship tracking must beat the baseline significantly to be included; it is not. The agents' average and spread for the same months come from an earlier run of 20 October 2026 that estimated the flag-basis total only (tracker/README.md).

Ship tracking (AIS): how it is made

Signals and vessels

Global Fishing Watch receives ships' AIS broadcasts and publishes fishing events, port visits and encounters (two vessels close together at sea) through its Events API; identities come from its Vessels API. The vessel list is the WCPFC Record of Fishing Vessels for the Kiribati, Nauru and Tuvalu flags, matched to a Global Fishing Watch identity by radio call sign, then IMO number (20 October 2026 build: 116 entries, 83 matched). "Kiribati-flagged" is the flag Global Fishing Watch reports, or, where that is not one of the three, the register flag.

Registration windows

A Global Fishing Watch identity covers the whole life of a hull. Each event is kept only if it falls inside a period when the vessel's own AIS reported one of the three flags, or, failing that, inside its authorisation dates on the register. This trimmed earlier history under other flags, mostly in Russian waters, and only 0.2% of hours inside Kiribati's waters (tracker/PROVENANCE.md).

What the counts measure

Fishing hours are time in which a vessel's movement looks like fishing: effort, not catch. AIS reception improves over time and differs by sea area, so part of a rise can be better detection. A port call is a detected visit, assigned to the country of the port; not an unloading, and silent on tonnage. For each encounter between a catcher and a carrier the tracker follows the carrier's next port calls and notes the first at a Thai port within 120 days; the share and the median lag show where carriers went, not what they held.

Why no value is drawn from the hours

Monthly fishing hours do not predict the monthly value importers record: in levels the series trend in opposite directions, and in 12-month changes the fit is negligible (R² about 0.02; tracker/PROVENANCE.md, GPS/out/bridge_fit.csv). The fleet page shows the two side by side and draws no estimate from either. Months with fewer than three partners published are drawn lighter and kept out of such comparisons.

Vessel register: how the counts are made

The Vessel register page counts the entries the Kiribati, Nauru and Tuvalu flag states submitted to the WCPFC Record of Fishing Vessels, as exported on the date shown. Deleted vessels stay in the export with the flag state's reason and are counted as deleted. Types are grouped from the register's FAO codes. The address hint is a text pattern on the owner, operator and charterer fields: an address in one of the three states counts as "at home", elsewhere as "abroad", both as "mixed", none as "no usable address". Only counts leave the working files; names and identifiers never do (tracker/build_registry_data.py; the tests scan the published file).

Knowledge base: how the inventory is made

The Knowledge base page counts the documents the agents can read, by type and by the country they are about: statistical manuals and standards; customs rules; data documentation; academic papers; IMF and other official reports; the author's project briefs; news. Words are counted in the text extracted from each file; pages without a text layer count as zero. "General" holds material that applies to every country. The date a document was added is the date of its converted text file. No document text is published (tracker/build_corpus_data.py).

Validation: how the agents will be tested

The Validation page describes a hold-out test. For island economies with tuna fleets and a published balance of payments, an agent receives everything except the published goods-export figure for one year and predicts it from its principles and the other evidence; a program scores the prediction against the published figure. Principles may be revised for at most three logged rounds; a reserved set of country-years is revealed only after the principles are frozen; the frozen principles are the ones applied to Kiribati. The panel is empty and the success measure is not yet set. Published figures of panel countries are cited by vintage and not assumed to be right.
