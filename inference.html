The question and the method

Importing countries record tuna as Kiribati-origin by the flag of the catching vessel. The balance of payments counts a sale as a Kiribati export only if the vessel's operator is resident in Kiribati. The question is the value of true exports: the FOB value of fish that Kiribati residents caught and sold to non-residents, whatever the flag, whatever the route, and whether or not any statistic recorded it. The Kiribati National Statistics Office measures it under the balance-of-payments rule from the returns it receives; the estimate here is a second reading of the same quantity, built from partner data and public evidence, offered to help that work.

The tracker asks thirty AI agents, the "experts". Each agent is one fresh session of a large language model, with no memory of the other sessions. Each receives the same evidence pack (the partner series, fleet and register aggregates, the statistical standards, Kiribati's official figures) and the same instructions, and works alone. Each first writes numbered principles that connect the evidence to the question, then freezes them, then walks from the raw partner figure to its estimate in six signed layers, each with a range and a reason, then audits its own answer. A program checks every answer (the layers reconcile, the ranges are ordered, no banned words) and computes the statistics shown here. The agents do no arithmetic that is published.

The spread across the thirty is the room for judgment that the evidence leaves. It is not a standard error: the agents share one evidence pack and one underlying model, so what none of them knows does not show.

          Early estimate of the partner series itself

          A different, smaller question: what will importers record for months they have not yet published? Thailand, the main buyer, publishes with a lag of several months. A statistical baseline fills the gap from what has been published, and the agents of an earlier run gave their own view of the same total. This is an estimate of the flag-basis figure, not of true exports.

          What this is, and is not

          It is a stated theory, written down before the numbers, applied without exception to the evidence by thirty independent readers, with the disagreement shown. Every layer has a reason, and every reason cites the evidence it rests on.

          It is not an official estimate, and it is not scored against the official figure: the two answer the same question from different evidence, and the estimate can sit above or below the published figure without either being wrong. Whether the agents understand how a published figure is built is a separate test, under construction on the Validation page.
