The question in two numbers

…

USD million of tuna that importing countries recorded as coming from Kiribati in …. Source: UN Comtrade, partner returns.

…

USD million of goods exports in Kiribati's balance of payments in …. Source: IMF.

Both figures are correct records of different things. The tracker explains the difference and estimates what lies behind it.

The question

Every shipment is recorded twice: once by the country it leaves and once by the country it enters. Kiribati's own records are the official ones. This tracker reads the other record, what Kiribati's trading partners declared to UN Comtrade, the United Nations trade database, and uses it to say something about Kiribati's economy earlier and from a second angle.

For tuna the two records differ by a large multiple, and the difference is a matter of definition. Importing countries credit a fish to the country whose flag the catching vessel flies. The balance of payments credits a sale to the country where the vessel's operator is resident. A purse seiner on the Kiribati register run from abroad produces "Kiribati" tuna in the first record and none in the second. The public vessel register gives a usable owner address for fewer than half of the vessels on the Kiribati, Nauru and Tuvalu flags, so neither record settles how much of the catch belongs to Kiribati residents.

The question the tracker asks is therefore: what is the true value of the tuna that Kiribati residents sell abroad? The Kiribati National Statistics Office measures it under the balance-of-payments rule, from the returns it receives. The tracker offers a second reading of the same quantity, built from partner data and public evidence, to help that work: it gathers five kinds of evidence, shows what each can and cannot see, and asks thirty AI agents to reason from the evidence to an estimate, one layer at a time. The page is built in the same order: the path of a fish, the five inputs, the inference, and how it will be tested.

How a fish becomes a statistic

Follow one tuna from the sea to the published figures. Each step leaves a trace somewhere, but no single source sees the whole path. The marks show what the tracker can and cannot observe at each step.

Seen by ship-tracking (AIS), Global Fishing Watch Recorded in official trade or customs data Not observed by the tracker
  1. 1

    Catch

    Purse-seine and longline vessels on the Kiribati register fish in Kiribati's waters and in neighbouring Pacific waters.

    • Fishing hours. Hours spent fishing, from AIS. This is effort, not catch.
    • Vessel register. The WCPFC register says which vessels are on the Kiribati flag.
    • Not observed: tonnes caught.
  2. 2

    Hand-over at sea

    The catcher stays on the fishing grounds. It passes its catch to a refrigerated carrier vessel that comes alongside.

    • Meetings at sea. Vessel pairs seen together, from AIS.
    • Not observed: what is transferred, or how much.
  3. 3

    Carrier to port

    The carrier sails to a port, often Bangkok, and unloads. The catcher itself usually does not make this trip.

    • Port visits. Carrier calls, from AIS.
    • Not observed: tonnage unloaded, or whether it is the same fish.
  4. 4

    Import record

    The importer's customs records the arrival. Under its rules of origin, fish caught at sea is credited to the country whose flag the catching vessel flies.

    • Partner-reported imports in UN Comtrade: USD value of tuna "from Kiribati". This is the main tracker.
    • Timing: published about four months after the month.
  5. 5

    Kiribati's own books

    Kiribati records its own exports. Official statistics are the reference.

    • Kiribati customs returns in UN Comtrade: published late and with gaps.
    • Balance of payments (IMF): goods exports, annual.

Two ways of recording the same fish

Trade statistics (step 4) ask which country's vessel caught the fish, and credit that country as the origin.

The balance of payments (step 5) asks who owns the fish when it is sold abroad. If the operator is resident in Kiribati, the sale is a Kiribati export; if the operator is resident elsewhere, it is not.

These are different questions, so the two records can differ without either one being wrong. The tracker does not say which applies to any vessel.

What the inputs add to one another

The AIS steps (1 to 3) arrive within days. The import record (step 4) arrives months later and the official figures (step 5) later still. Read together, they show where activity is happening before the trade figures are in, and which steps have no measurement at all. The inference section below starts from step 4 and reasons toward the value that step 5 is meant to capture.

The evidence: five inputs

Each card shows one chart from the input, says what it can and cannot tell, and links to the page that holds the full detail.

Trading-partner records (UN Comtrade) →

What importing countries declared they bought from Kiribati, by product and month, published about four months after the fact. Tuna is credited to Kiribati by the flag of the catching vessel. It tells when, to whom and what. It does not tell who operated the vessel.

Ship tracking (AIS): Kiribati-flagged vessels →

Positions that vessels broadcast, collected by Global Fishing Watch. The chart counts the hours that vessels on the Kiribati flag were seen fishing, by year. The full page adds when they met carrier vessels at sea and which ports the carriers visited, within days of the event. None of it shows tonnes caught or value sold.

Open the ship-tracking page →

Source: Global Fishing Watch, fishing events for Kiribati-flagged vessels on the WCPFC register, counted only inside each vessel's time on the register.

Vessel register (WCPFC) →

The public list of vessels the Kiribati, Nauru and Tuvalu flag states have authorised to fish, with vessel type, build year, previous flag and an owner address. The address is a hint about where the operator is based. Residence, the condition that decides whether a catch is a Kiribati export, is not on the register.

Open the vessel-register page →

Source: WCPFC Record of Fishing Vessels, export of …. Counts only; no names are published.

Official statistics (Kiribati) →

Kiribati's balance of payments, compiled with the IMF, and its customs returns on UN Comtrade. They are the reference for the country's accounts. Goods exports follow the residence rule. The agents may read these figures as evidence of what the compiler recorded; they are not the target of the estimate.

AI-based inference: from the partner figure to true exports

Thirty AI agents, the "experts", receive the same evidence and the same instructions and work alone. Each agent is one fresh session of a large language model, with no memory of the other sessions. Each writes down its principles, then walks from the raw partner figure to its own estimate of true exports in six steps, then checks itself. A program, not an agent, aggregates the thirty answers. The spread across agents shows how much room for judgment the evidence leaves; it is not a standard error.

The full detail, the six layers with each agent's reasons, the principles the agents wrote and how they used the official figures, is on the Inference page. The method is in Methods.
