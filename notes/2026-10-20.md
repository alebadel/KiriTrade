# Nowcast for June to November 2026 — 20 October 2026

Kiribati tuna nowcast, 20 October 2026. This note covers the six months June to November 2026. The quantity nowcast is partner-reported tuna trade, attributed by the flag of the catching vessel, in millions of US dollars. Thirty agent draws were run, each given the same evidence, and all thirty were accepted. The mean total is 5.07 for June, 4.79 for July, 4.683 for August, 4.707 for September, 4.697 for October and 4.687 for November. Between agents, the 10th to 90th percentile range is 4.79 to 5.42 in June and 4.5 to 5.0 in November.

The baseline model gives higher figures. Its point estimates are 6.171 for June, 5.836 for July and 5.517 for each of August to November, with 80 percent bands running from 1.909 to 9.587 in June and 0.441 to 9.541 for the months with no partner returns yet. The agents sit below the baseline in every month. The gap is widest in June, where the agent mean is 5.07 against 6.171. Agents also disagree with each other most in June and July, where the standard deviation is about 0.30, against about 0.22 from August on. Three-month out-of-sample error for the baseline's partner-completion model is 6.527. An alternative using vessel-tracking arrivals scored 6.435 but did not pass the pre-set test against it (Diebold-Mariano statistic 0.26, not significant), so tracking data is context for the agents and not a model input.

The split into Component A and Component B rests mainly on three principles. First, all thirty agents note that the partner series follows the vessel's flag, so it measures flag-state catch, not who operates the vessel or owns the fish. Second, all thirty note that a goods export requires a change of ownership from a resident to a nonresident. Third, all thirty set the resident-operator share as a moderate minority, held constant across months, because the register address hints and fishing-hours trend are only weak signals. Component A, the resident-operator part, averages 1.195 in June and about 1.1 thereafter, a share of 0.235 of the total. Component B, the remainder, averages 3.875 in June and about 3.6 afterwards.

What the evidence cannot tell us matters. The spread between agents is disagreement given identical evidence. It is not a standard error and should not be read as a confidence interval. The split between Component A and Component B rests on an assumed share and is not measured in the evidence. Partner reporting is lagged and incomplete for recent months, and the main buyer's figures are unpublished. Processing arrangements and at-sea transfers cannot be checked from the available evidence, so no adjustment is made for them.
