Ship tracking (AIS)

Where Kiribati-flagged fishing vessels were seen at sea and in port, from the position signals ships broadcast (AIS). The signals arrive within days, long before partner trade figures. Input 2 of 5 on the home page.

Activity only: hours, meetings at sea, port calls. No catch, exports or value. In the inference, the agents read these as evidence on how much of the Kiribati-flag catch belongs to resident operators (layer "Less: catch of non-resident operators").

What this page shows

Large ships broadcast their identity and position (AIS). Global Fishing Watch collects the signals and detects when a vessel is fishing, meets another vessel at sea, or visits a port.

This page counts those detections for vessels on the Kiribati flag, only while each was on the register. One fact, the at-sea chain, also covers the Nauru and Tuvalu flags, which share operators with Kiribati's; it says so. The diagram on the home page shows where each step of a fish's path is, and is not, measured.

Every number is an observation of where vessels were, not of what they caught, sold or earned.

Words used on this page
AIS
The position beacon ships broadcast, picked up by satellite and collected by Global Fishing Watch.
Flag, Kiribati-flagged
The country whose register a ship is on and whose rules it sails under. Not where its operator lives.
Register
The flag state's list of vessels it authorises to fish, kept by the WCPFC (Western and Central Pacific Fisheries Commission).
Fishing hours (effort, not catch)
Hours in which AIS shows a vessel moving as it does when fishing. More hours need not mean more fish.
Encounter
Two ships seen close together at sea, typically a catcher and a refrigerated carrier. AIS cannot show whether fish moved between them.
Port call
A port stop seen by AIS. Not an unloading.
Median, middle half
The median is the middle value; the middle half runs from the 25th to the 75th percentile.
Partner-reported
What importing countries record in UN Comtrade about goods from Kiribati. See the Partner data page.

What this page does not show

  • It shows no vessel or company names, and no individual ship's track. Only totals are published.
  • It makes no statement about who owns or operates the vessels. A flag is not residence: whether an operator is resident in Kiribati or abroad is a separate question that this page does not address.
  • It does not estimate catch, exports or earnings from the AIS signals. Official Kiribati statistics remain the reference for those.
  • It does not compare AIS activity with any fees or government revenue.

How these numbers are made, and what they can and cannot measure, is explained in How the numbers are made.
