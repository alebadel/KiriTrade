Validation design set, no scores yet

How the tracker will test whether its AI agents understand how a published goods-export figure is built. The design is set; the panel of test countries is empty; there are no scores yet.

What can and cannot be tested

The inference estimates the value of the tuna that Kiribati residents sell abroad, a quantity the Kiribati National Statistics Office measures inside its goods-exports line from the returns it receives. The estimate is a second reading from different evidence, so the official figure is not used to score it. What can be scored is the agents' understanding of the machinery: how a statistics office turns catch, flags, ownership, transshipment and importers' records into the goods-export figure it publishes. If the agents can reproduce other countries' published figures from the other evidence, their principles are doing real work; if not, the Kiribati estimate rests on principles that fail where they can be checked.

The test

  1. Panel (candidates, not yet screened). Island economies with tuna fleets and a published balance of payments: first the Pacific states (Solomon Islands, Papua New Guinea, Micronesia, Marshall Islands, Tuvalu, Nauru, Fiji), then Indian Ocean states, and Caribbean states where national statistics and partner reporting are better. Each enters with its partner series, its register where one exists, its documented compilation practice and the vintage of its official figures.
  2. Hold-out. For each country-year, the agent receives everything except the published goods-export figure for that year. Nothing about that year's figure is in its evidence. It predicts the figure, with a written derivation that uses only its stated principles and the pack.
  3. Score. A program compares the prediction with the published figure and records the error. The success measure is not yet set; it will be fixed before the first panel run. The agent may revise its principles for at most three rounds, each logged; a set of country-years is kept back and revealed only after the principles are frozen.
  4. Carry over. The frozen principles are the ones applied to Kiribati. A principle that fails on the panel is not rescued for Kiribati.

Two limits are stated now. The published figures of panel countries are not assumed to be right; they are what their compilers produced, cited by vintage. And a good score on the panel shows that the agents can reproduce compilers' practice, not that the practice equals the true value; the Kiribati question remains an estimate under stated conditions.

Limit
A panel result that contradicts a principle used in the Kiribati bridge, above all on the share of catch that belongs to non-resident operators, would change the Kiribati estimate.
