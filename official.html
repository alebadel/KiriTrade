What these figures are

Two official sources describe Kiribati's trade. The balance of payments records the country's transactions with the rest of the world by the rules of the IMF's Balance of Payments Manual, sixth edition (BPM6). Its goods-exports line counts goods whose ownership passed from a Kiribati resident to a non-resident, valued at the border (FOB). It is annual and compiled by the Kiribati authorities with the IMF. Customs returns are what Kiribati's customs office records as leaving and entering the country, by product and month, published to UN Comtrade for the months the office has processed.

For fish caught at sea the balance of payments follows the residence of the vessel's operator, not the vessel's flag. Fish caught by a Kiribati-flagged vessel run by a non-resident operator is not a Kiribati export under this rule, and the goods-exports line is built on that basis. Importing countries follow the flag. That is why the goods-exports line here is small next to the tuna that partners record as Kiribati-origin on the Partner data page: the two series answer different questions.

Fishing access fees that foreign fleets pay Kiribati are recorded in the balance of payments as income, a separate flow from goods. They are not shown on this page and are never compared with the tuna series on this site, because one does not explain the other.

How the tracker uses these figures. They are shown beside the partner data and the agents' estimates for reference. The agents may read them as evidence of what the compiler recorded and of the compiler's coverage. They are not the target of the estimate: the question on the home page is the true value of resident tuna exports, which can sit above or below any published figure.
